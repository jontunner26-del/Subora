How to check a vendor on Subora before you pay: ticks, trust score and ratings explained

Before you pay for a digital product, it helps to know who you are buying from. Every Subora vendor has a page that shows what was checked and how past buyers rated them. This guide explains what each signal means, what it does not mean, and which questions to ask in chat before you pay.

By Subora Editorial Team · Published 5 November 2026 · Updated 5 November 2026 · 7 min read

Key takeaways

  • Subora approves vendors by hand. They give identity documents, a selfie and bank details before they can sell, and every listing is reviewed before it goes live[1].
  • A blue tick marks a verified vendor. A gold tick marks a listing sold directly by Subora[1]. A tick shows that checks were done, not that one product will work.
  • A vendor’s page shows how long they have sold, how many sales they have made, how fast they reply, their rating, and a trust score that Subora calculates and vendors cannot edit.
  • Ask your questions in the Subora chat before you pay. A clear, quick answer is a good sign. A push to pay outside Subora is a red flag[1].
  • Escrow protects your payment even if a vendor is new. Pay inside Subora, test your access, and confirm only when it works[2].

The short answer

Look at five things before you pay: the vendor’s verification label, how long they have been selling, their rating and its spread, their trust score, and how they answer a question in chat. None of these proves a particular product will work for you. Together they tell you how much evidence there is that this vendor delivers.

This guide is editorial advice from our team. The vendor checks it describes are Subora’s published rules. The description of the vendor page reflects the app as it stands on 5 November 2026 and may change. For a checklist about the listing itself, read our guide to checking a digital product listing.

What Subora checks before a vendor can sell

Vendors apply and are approved by hand. They give identity documents, a selfie and bank details before they can sell, and every listing is reviewed before it goes live. Payouts only go to a bank account in the vendor’s own verified name[1].

Sellers of gift cards, crypto, accounts and numbers carry more risk, so they have extra steps: identity verification, a video selfie, a proof of address and a ₦100,000 security deposit. Confirmed claims give a seller strikes. Three confirmed claims pause their listings, five suspend the account, and fraud closes it and uses the deposit to repay buyers[1].

What the ticks and labels mean

  • Blue tick. A verified vendor who passed Subora’s identity and bank checks[1].
  • Gold tick. A listing sold directly by Subora. The vendor page shows it as an official Subora store[1].
  • Not verified. A vendor who has not completed identity verification yet. The page says so, and also notes that escrow still protects your payment until you confirm that your access works.

Be realistic about what a tick tells you. It tells you the checks were done. It does not tell you that this specific product will work for you, so keep reading the page.

How to read a vendor’s page

Tap a vendor’s name on any listing to open their page. Here is what each signal tells you, and what it does not.

SignalWhat it tells youWhat it does not tell you
Verified labelIdentity and bank checks were passedThat every product will work
Selling sinceHow long the vendor has been on SuboraThat a long history means good service today
Sales countHow many orders the vendor has completedHow many of those buyers were happy
Response timeHow quickly the vendor usually repliesWhether the answers are clear and correct
Rating out of 5How buyers scored the vendor, with the share at each star levelMuch, if there are only a few ratings
Trust scoreA score Subora calculates from the last 90 days of deliveries, disputes and ratings. Vendors cannot edit itAnything about older history
Orders without a dispute, and rated 4 stars or aboveTwo percentages that show how often orders go smoothlyAnything for a new vendor, where the page says there is no data yet

Read the rating spread, not just the average

A 4.8 average can hide a pattern. Look at the bars for each star level. A small share of low ratings is normal for any seller. A cluster of one-star ratings is worth reading about. If the vendor has only a few ratings, treat the average as weak evidence.

Use the trust score as a recent snapshot

The trust score looks only at the last 90 days, so it shows how the vendor is doing now. Next to the score, Subora shows a label such as Excellent or Building trust. A new vendor with a lower score is not a bad vendor. It simply means there is less evidence, so read the other signals closely and start with a smaller order.

Ask in the chat before you pay

You can chat with a vendor before and after you buy, and every message is kept as a permanent record[1][2]. Use the chat for one short test. Ask what you will receive and how, how long it lasts, how many devices or screens are allowed, and what happens if the provider changes the plan.

Good signs are a clear answer, a reasonable reply time, and willingness to put the terms in writing. Red flags are vague replies, pressure to hurry, a request for your password or a code, and any push to pay or talk outside Subora. Payments outside Subora have no escrow, no refunds and no dispute help[3]. On Subora, a chat message that asks you to pay or talk outside can be reported, and it is hidden and reviewed. Subora staff never ask for your password, your codes or payment outside the app[1]. Read our guide to escrow versus paying a seller directly to see what you give up by paying outside.

When a vendor page shows a warning

If a vendor is suspended or closed, their page says Subora is reviewing claims against them, and their listings are hidden. If you see this, do not look for a way around it, and do not accept an offer to continue the deal outside Subora. Report anything that looks wrong, using the Report vendor button on the page.

What checking a vendor cannot do

A good vendor page lowers your risk, but it cannot remove every risk. Some providers, such as streaming, AI and software companies, have their own rules about sharing accounts, and a provider can change or end access at any time. If that happens, the refund rules apply[1]. Each vendor is responsible for checking those rules before listing. Also, the vendor is paid after you confirm, or after 48 hours with no problem reported, so test your access straight away[2].

After you choose a vendor

Pay from your Subora wallet, test your login, code or link, and tap confirm only when it works. If it does not, tap Report a problem on the subscription card. Read how escrow works, step by step and how Subora works. If a vendor or a business does not resolve a complaint, Nigeria’s Federal Competition and Consumer Protection Commission takes complaints through its website[5]. When you are ready, browse the marketplace with these checks in hand[4].

Frequently asked questions

How do I know a Subora vendor is legitimate?

Check the verification label, how long they have sold, their rating and its spread, and their trust score, then ask a question in chat. Subora approves vendors by hand with identity documents, a selfie and bank details[1], but no signal guarantees one product will work.

What does the blue tick mean?

A blue tick marks a verified vendor who passed Subora’s identity and bank checks. A gold tick marks a listing sold directly by Subora[1]. A tick shows checks were done, not that a given product will work.

Is a vendor with few or no ratings risky?

Not necessarily, but there is less evidence. Read the other signals, ask a question in chat, and consider a smaller first order. Escrow still holds your payment until you confirm that your access works[2].

What is the trust score, and can vendors change it?

It is a score that Subora calculates from the last 90 days of deliveries, disputes and ratings. Vendors cannot edit it. It is a recent snapshot, not a lifetime record.

What should I do if a vendor asks me to pay outside Subora?

Do not pay outside. Payments outside Subora have no escrow, no refunds and no dispute help[3]. Report the message, which will be hidden and reviewed[1].

How do I report a vendor?

Use the Report vendor button on the vendor’s page, or report a chat message. Subora reviews reports and can pause or suspend a vendor[1]. For an unresolved consumer complaint, you can also contact the FCCPC[5].
