How to check a digital product listing before you buy: a 7-step checklist

Most problems with digital products can be spotted in the listing, before any money moves. This checklist walks you through seven questions to ask about the category, delivery, access limits, vendor and payment, and tells you when it is safe to confirm that you have received what you paid for.

By Subora Editorial Team · Published 3 November 2026 · Updated 3 November 2026 · 8 min read

Key takeaways

  • Check seven things before you pay: the category, what is delivered, the access limits, the vendor, the price, where your payment sits, and how you will confirm receipt.
  • Claim windows differ by category: 48 hours for subscriptions and courses, 30 minutes for gift cards, 1 hour for crypto, 24 hours for accounts and 20 minutes for OTP numbers[3].
  • A tick next to a vendor shows the checks they passed. It does not prove that one particular product will work[1].
  • Pay only inside the platform. Payments outside Subora have no escrow, no refunds and no dispute help[2].
  • Test your access straight away. If you do nothing for 48 hours, the vendor is paid[1].

Why check the listing, not just the seller

Our guide to digital product scams and escrow covers the warning signs in a seller. This guide goes one level closer, to the listing itself. A listing is the promise: what you will get, for how long, under which rules and from whom. If the promise is vague, you cannot hold anyone to it.

The check takes a few minutes and costs nothing. It matters because once you pay, your protection is the escrow process and a claim window that can be as short as 20 minutes[1][3]. The seven checks below are editorial guidance from our team. The rules they refer to, such as claim windows and vendor checks, are Subora’s published rules.

The seven checks

CheckThe question to ask
1. CategoryWhat kind of product is this, and which rules apply to it?
2. DeliveryWhat exactly will I receive, and how?
3. Access limitsHow long, on how many screens or seats, and under whose rules?
4. VendorWho is selling, and what has been checked?
5. PriceWhy is it this price, and what does a longer plan change?
6. Payment statusWhere is my money after I pay?
7. Confirm receiptHow do I test it, and when do I tap confirm?

1. Name the category

Decide what you are buying: a place on a subscription, a course or download, a gift card, crypto, an account or a virtual number. The category matters because the rules and the clock are different for each one. Subscriptions and seats give the vendor 48 hours to fix or refund. Courses and downloads are refundable if the files do not open or do not match the description, reported within 48 hours. Gift cards, crypto, accounts and OTP numbers have much shorter windows, listed in step 7[3].

2. Find out what is delivered, and how

A good listing says what you receive: a sign-in code, an access link, a file, a login. Many Subora listings deliver automatically, so a code or link arrives on your subscription card within seconds, with no wait for the vendor[2]. Others are delivered by the vendor through chat. If the listing does not say which, ask before you buy. Subora lets you chat with a vendor before and after you buy, and every message is kept on record[2][5].

3. Read the access limits

Look for four things: the plan length, how many screens or seats you get, any rules of use, and whether the provider allows this kind of sharing. Each vendor states what you get and the rules of use, and you accept them before you pay[2]. Provider rules are the part people skip. OpenAI’s consumer terms, for example, say you may not share your account credentials or make your account available to anyone else[6]. Subora’s terms say that some providers do not allow sharing, that vendors must check this before listing, and that a provider can change or end access at any time, in which case the refund rules apply[4]. Ask the vendor what happens if access ends part-way through your plan.

4. Check who is selling and what was checked

On Subora, vendors are approved by hand and give identity documents, a selfie and bank details before they can sell. A blue tick marks a verified vendor, and a gold tick marks a listing sold directly by Subora. Payouts go only to a bank account in the vendor’s own verified name[1]. Sellers of gift cards, crypto, accounts and numbers carry more risk, so they also complete a video selfie, a proof of address and a ₦100,000 security deposit, and they receive strikes for confirmed claims[1].

Be realistic about what a tick tells you. It tells you the checks were done. It does not tell you that this specific product will work for you, so also look at the vendor’s page and any reviews, and note how clearly and quickly they answer a question in chat.

5. Question the price and the plan length

Compare the price with the provider’s official price, which you can find on the provider’s own site. A discount on a shared plan is normal, because the cost is split. A discount that cannot be explained is a warning sign. On most subscriptions you can buy several months at once, and longer plans cost less per month[2]. A longer plan is also a longer commitment to the access staying available, so read the vendor’s terms for what happens if it does not.

6. Know where your money is after you pay

Pay from your Subora wallet, never to a personal account. When you pay, Subora holds the money instead of sending it to the vendor, and the vendor is paid once you confirm your access works, or after 48 hours with no reported problem[1][2]. This is why you cannot pay a vendor directly: payments outside Subora have no escrow, no refunds and no dispute help[5]. If a chat message asks you to pay or talk outside the app, do not reply. Report it. Those messages are hidden and reviewed[1].

7. Test it, then confirm

Confirm only when your login, code or link works. Do it straight away, because the claim clock starts early and the 48-hour release is automatic[2]. If something is wrong, tap Report a problem on the subscription card. The vendor has 48 hours to respond while your payment stays frozen in escrow. A refund can be full, for the days you did not use, or a custom amount, and it returns to your Subora wallet straight away[1].

The vague listing breaks more than one check at once, which is what makes it easy to spot. The clear one is not guaranteed to work, but it gives you something specific to test and a record to point to if it does not.

The checklist on one screen

  1. Name the category and note its claim window[3].
  2. Confirm what is delivered and how. Ask in chat if it is not stated.
  3. Read the access limits: length, screens, rules and provider policy[4].
  4. Check the vendor: tick, page, reviews and how they answer[1].
  5. Question the price against the official price.
  6. Pay only inside the platform, from your wallet[5].
  7. Test straight away, then confirm only if it works[2].

If it still goes wrong

Use Report a problem on the card first, and use support chat if the vendor does not reply. Chats, payments and dispute statements are permanent records, so they can be used to settle the case[1]. If a business does not resolve a consumer complaint, Nigeria’s Federal Competition and Consumer Protection Commission takes complaints through its website[7]. Frequently asked questions

How do I check if a digital product listing is legitimate?

Work through seven questions: the category, what is delivered, the access limits, the vendor, the price, where your payment sits, and how you will confirm receipt. A legitimate listing answers all seven clearly, and a vague one rarely does.

What should a good listing include?

It should state what you receive and how, the plan length, any rules of use, and who the vendor is. On Subora, vendors state what you get and the rules of use, and you accept them before you pay[2].

What does the blue tick mean?

A blue tick marks a verified vendor who passed Subora’s identity and bank checks. A gold tick marks a listing sold directly by Subora[1]. A tick shows checks were done, not that a given product will work.

When should I tap confirm?

Only after you have tested your login, code or link and it works. The vendor is paid once you confirm, or after 48 hours with no problem reported[2].

How long do I have to report a problem?

It depends on the category: 48 hours for subscriptions and courses, 30 minutes for gift cards after revealing the code, 1 hour for crypto, 24 hours for accounts and 20 minutes for OTP numbers[3].

Can I get a refund if I pay the seller directly?

Not through Subora. Payments made outside Subora have no escrow, no refunds and no dispute help[5]. Call your bank straight away if you have already paid.
