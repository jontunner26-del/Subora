My shared subscription stopped working: what to do and how to get a refund

A shared subscription that worked yesterday and fails today is annoying, and it usually has a simple cause. This guide shows what to check in the first few minutes, how to report the problem on Subora, how refunds are worked out, and why the 48-hour rule means you should not wait.

By Subora Editorial Team · Published 5 November 2026 · Updated 5 November 2026 · 7 min read

Key takeaways

  • Test your access as soon as it arrives and report any problem at once. The vendor is paid after 48 hours with no problem reported[2].
  • Tap Report a problem on the subscription card. The vendor has 48 hours to respond, and your payment stays frozen in escrow while they do[1].
  • A refund can be full, prorated for the days you did not use, or a custom amount. It goes back to your Subora wallet straight away[3].
  • If the vendor does not reply, you can send an urgent nudge by SMS and email, and Subora support steps in if there is still no answer[2].
  • A provider can change or end access at any time. If that happens, the refund rules apply[1][4].

The short answer

Do three things, in this order. First, spend two minutes checking the obvious causes below. Second, if access still fails, tap Report a problem on your subscription card straight away. Third, keep every message inside the Subora chat, so there is one permanent record of what was said and paid[1].

The key point is speed. On Subora the vendor is paid when you confirm that your access works, or after 48 hours if you have not reported a problem[2]. A problem reported early is much easier to settle than one reported late. This guide is editorial advice from our team. The refund and dispute rules it describes are Subora’s published rules.

Why shared access stops working

We cannot see inside each provider’s systems, so treat these as common possibilities, not diagnoses.

  • The sign-in details changed. A password, sign-in code or access link was updated, and the one you saved is out of date.
  • Too many devices or locations. Many plans limit how many devices or places can use one login at the same time.
  • The provider enforces its sharing rules. Some providers do not allow sharing at all. OpenAI’s consumer terms, for example, say you may not share your account credentials or make your account available to anyone else[6]. Spotify’s family plan terms set a same-address requirement for members[7].
  • The plan ended or was paused. The shared plan may have lapsed on the provider’s side.
  • The vendor removed or changed your seat. This is a reason to contact the vendor straight away.

Some of these can be fixed in minutes by the vendor. Others, such as a provider ending sharing, cannot. Either way, the reporting steps are the same.

Five quick checks before you report

  1. Use the latest details. Open your subscription card and use the newest login, code or link shown there, not an old message.
  2. Try one device. Sign out of other devices if the plan has a device limit, then try again on a single device.
  3. Note the exact error. Take a screenshot of the message you see, with the time. This helps the vendor and, if needed, Subora support.
  4. Re-read the vendor’s rules of use. Each vendor states what you get and the rules of use, and you accept them before you pay[2]. A rule about devices or hours may explain the problem.
  5. Leave the account settings alone. Do not change the password or settings on a shared login unless the vendor’s terms tell you to. It can lock other members out and make it harder to see what happened.

How to report it on Subora

Open the subscription card for that order and tap Report a problem. Describe what you see in plain words: the error message, when it started and what you already tried. The vendor has 48 hours to respond, and your payment stays frozen in escrow while they do[1].

You can also chat with the vendor from the same order. Keep the conversation in the Subora chat. Chats, wallet entries and dispute statements cannot be edited or deleted, and a message that asks you to pay or talk outside Subora can be reported and is hidden and reviewed[1].

How refunds are worked out

For subscriptions and seats, a refund can be full, or prorated for the days you did not use. In some cases a custom amount is agreed[1][3]. Every refund is recorded in both wallets and goes back to your Subora wallet straight away. You can spend it on another listing or ask support to send it to your bank[3].

Here is an invented example. You pay ₦6,000 for 30 days, and the access stops working after 10 days. A prorated refund could cover the 20 unused days, which is about ₦4,000. The amount agreed in a real case may differ, because it depends on what the vendor and Subora decide from the record.

If the vendor does not reply

If the vendor is away, you can send an urgent nudge by SMS and email. Subora support steps in if there is still no reply[2]. You can also reach support through live chat in the app, from the Help Centre, every day from 8am to 10pm West Africa Time, or by email at support@subora.com.ng[5].

If you and the vendor disagree

If you do not agree on the outcome, Subora decides using the full chat and payment record, writes down the reason, and decides within 48 hours of both sides sharing their evidence[1]. This is why a clear description and saved screenshots matter. They are the evidence.

What if the provider ended sharing?

Subora is a marketplace. It provides the place where buyers and vendors meet, and it does not own, supply or control what vendors list. Some providers do not allow sharing or reselling access, and each vendor is responsible for checking those rules before listing. A provider can also change or end access at any time. If that happens, the refund rules apply[1][4]. Escrow protects your payment. It cannot make a provider allow sharing.

Mistakes that make it harder

  • Waiting. The vendor is paid after 48 hours with no problem reported[2].
  • Confirming before you have tested. Confirm only when your login, code or link works.
  • Paying outside Subora to “fix” it. Payments outside Subora have no escrow, no refunds and no dispute help. Read our guide to escrow versus paying a seller directly.
  • Sharing your codes or password. Subora staff never ask for your password, your codes or payment outside the app[1].

If a business does not resolve a consumer complaint, Nigeria’s Federal Competition and Consumer Protection Commission takes complaints through its website[8]. For the full process, see how escrow works, step by step and the refund policy.

Frequently asked questions

How long do I have to report a problem with a shared subscription?

Report it from the subscription card as soon as it fails. The vendor is paid after 48 hours with no problem reported[2], and the refund policy lists the claim windows for each kind of listing[3].

Will I get my full money back?

It depends on the case. A refund can be full, prorated for the days you did not use, or a custom amount[3]. If you and the vendor disagree, Subora decides using the full record[1].

Where does the refund go?

Back to your Subora wallet straight away. You can spend it or ask support to send it to your bank[3].

What if the vendor does not reply?

You can send an urgent nudge by SMS and email, and Subora support steps in if there is still no reply[2].

What if the provider stopped allowing sharing?

A provider can change or end access at any time. If that happens, Subora’s refund rules apply[1][4]. Report it from the subscription card as usual.

Can I contact support instead of the vendor?

Yes. Use live chat in the app from the Help Centre, every day from 8am to 10pm West Africa Time, or email support@subora.com.ng[5].
