Can you share a subscription in Nigeria? How to read the provider’s rules before you buy

Many buyers in Nigeria meet shared subscriptions because dollar-priced services cost a lot in naira. Before you join one, there is a question that matters more than the price: does the provider allow it? The answer is different for every service and every plan. This guide shows what five well-known providers say, how to read a provider’s terms in ten minutes, and what to ask a vendor before you pay.

By Subora Editorial Team · Published 10 November 2026 · Updated 10 November 2026 · 10 min read

Key takeaways

  • There is no single answer. Some plans are built to be shared, some allow sharing only inside a household, and some forbid it completely[5][8][9][10].
  • OpenAI’s and Anthropic’s consumer terms both say you may not share your login or make your account available to anyone else[5][7].
  • Netflix says you may not share outside your household, but lets the account owner add an extra member on some plans[8]. Spotify’s Family plan is for people at the same address[9]. Microsoft 365 Family lets the owner invite up to five other people[10].
  • Check three things: which document governs your plan, which words in it deal with sharing, and which country and date the page applies to.
  • Subora is a marketplace and does not control what vendors list. The vendor must check the provider’s rules before listing, and a provider can change or end access at any time[1].

Can you share a subscription? The short answer

It depends on the provider and on the plan. “Sharing a subscription” covers very different things, and the rules for one do not carry over to another. In practice there are three groups:

  1. Sharing is part of the plan. The provider built a way to share and gave you a tool for it. Microsoft 365 Family is an example: the owner invites other people from their own Microsoft account, and each person signs in with their own account[10][11].
  2. Sharing is allowed, but only inside a limit. Spotify’s Family plan is meant for family members who live at the same address[9]. Netflix asks you to keep the account inside one household, and offers a paid extra member option on some plans[8].
  3. Sharing is not allowed. OpenAI’s consumer terms say you may not share your account credentials or make your account available to anyone else[5]. Anthropic’s consumer terms say much the same for Claude[7].

This guide is editorial advice from our team and is not legal advice. We describe what the providers’ own pages say on the date we read them. Terms change, so always read the current version yourself before you decide.

Two things stand out. First, the same company can have different rules for different plans, so “Microsoft allows sharing” is true of Family and not of Personal. Second, even where sharing is built in, it usually sits inside a defined group, such as a family group or an address. That is why reading the plan name matters as much as reading the brand.

How to read a provider’s rules in ten minutes

You do not need to read a whole legal document. Follow these steps.

  1. Find the right document. Providers publish several. A consumer terms page covers individual plans, a business or team terms page covers workplace plans, and some plans have their own page, like Spotify’s Family terms[9][12]. Use the one that matches the plan you are buying.
  2. Search the page for key words. Use your browser’s find tool for share, household, credentials, account available, same address, resell and lease. The sentence you need is usually near the part about creating an account.
  3. Look at the plan name. Individual, Duo, Family, Team and Business plans are treated differently. A rule that applies to one may not apply to another.
  4. Check the country and the date. Features such as Netflix’s extra member option depend on your country and package[8]. Look for an “effective” date at the top of the page and note it. OpenAI’s consumer terms we read show an effective date of 1 January 2026[5], and the Anthropic terms we read show 8 October 2025[7].
  5. Write down what you found. Save the link and the date. If a provider’s rule changes, you will know what it said when you bought.

Questions to ask a vendor before you pay

Once you know the provider’s position, the vendor’s answers become much easier to judge. On Subora you can chat with the vendor before you buy[3]. Use that chance.

  • Which exact plan is this? Compare the answer with the plan name on the listing. The “Plan and access” section on a listing shows the service, the plan, the access type and the duration.
  • How do I get access? A built-in invitation, a login, a sign-in code and an access link carry different levels of risk. Our guide to checking a digital product listing explains the signals to look for.
  • Does the provider allow this kind of sharing? A good vendor can answer directly. Under Subora’s terms, each vendor is responsible for checking these rules before listing[1].
  • What happens if access stops? Ask for the vendor’s own terms in writing. Every vendor’s rules appear on the listing, and you accept them before you pay[3].
  • Who controls the password or the account? If you share a login, someone else controls it. Do not use it for anything private or important.

Always keep the conversation on Subora and pay from your wallet. A payment outside the platform has no escrow, no refund and no dispute help[4]. Our article on escrow versus paying a seller directly explains why.

When it is better to buy your own plan

A shared slot can make sense for something you want to try, or for a tool you use lightly. It is a poor fit in four cases:

  • The account holds private or work data. OpenAI’s help centre warns that sharing logins can expose personal data and payment information[6]. A shared login means other people can reach what is stored in it.
  • You depend on it for income or study. If a provider ends sharing, you lose access with no warning. A provider can change or end access at any time[1].
  • The provider’s terms clearly forbid sharing. You would be relying on a rule being unenforced.
  • The price gap is small. If your own plan costs only a little more, the risk is not worth the saving.

Frequently asked questions

Can you share a subscription legally?

That depends on the provider’s terms and on the law, and we cannot give legal advice. What we can say is that providers set their own rules for sharing. OpenAI and Anthropic’s consumer terms forbid sharing logins[5][7], while Microsoft 365 Family has a built-in way to share[10]. If the question matters to you, ask a lawyer.

Is it safe to join a shared ChatGPT account?

OpenAI’s consumer terms say you may not share your credentials or make your account available to anyone else[5], and its help centre warns that shared logins can expose personal data and payment information[6]. Treat any shared login as something others can reach, and keep private information out of it.

Can I share Netflix with someone who does not live with me?

Netflix’s terms say not outside your household, but on some plans the account owner can add an extra member for a fee[8]. Whether it is available depends on your plan, package and country, so check the Netflix Help Centre for your account.

What is the difference between a family plan and a shared slot?

A family plan is a product from the provider that is meant for several people, usually with limits such as the same address[9] or an invitation system[10]. A shared slot is a place on a plan sold by a vendor. The provider’s rules for that plan still apply to it.

What happens if the provider stops allowing sharing?

A provider can change or end access at any time, and Subora’s refund rules then apply[1]. Report it from your subscription card as soon as it happens, and see our guide on what to do when a shared subscription stops working.

Does Subora check the provider’s rules for me?

No. Subora is a marketplace and does not own, supply or control what vendors list. Each vendor must check the provider’s rules before listing, and Subora removes listings it is told break them[1]. Do your own check as well, using the steps above.
