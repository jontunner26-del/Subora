What can I buy on Subora? A practical category-by-category guide

Subora is a Nigerian marketplace for shared subscriptions, courses, software seats and other digital goods, priced in naira. This guide explains each category in plain words, what to check before you buy from it, and what no guide can promise: what is in stock today.

By Subora Editorial Team · Published 3 November 2026 · Updated 3 November 2026 · 8 min read

Key takeaways

  • Subora groups its listings into five areas: AI assistants, streaming and music, design and software, courses and templates, and accounts and numbers[1].
  • Gift cards and crypto are also covered by Subora’s safety and refund rules, and they need extra seller checks[2][3].
  • A category is a type of listing, not a stock list. Vendors add and remove listings, so open the marketplace to see what is available today[6].
  • Anyone can browse. You only need a free account when you are ready to buy[1].
  • Some providers forbid sharing logins[7][8]. Subora’s escrow protects your payment, but it cannot change a provider’s rules[4].

How to read this guide

Subora is a marketplace. Approved vendors list what they sell, every listing is reviewed before it goes live, and Subora also sells some official listings of its own[2][5]. Because the range depends on which vendors are active, this guide describes the kinds of thing you can buy, not a fixed catalogue.

To see what is available right now, open the Subora marketplace. You can browse and compare prices without an account. You sign up, for free, only when you want to buy or chat with a vendor[1][6].

The five main categories

Subora’s own guide to buying lists five groups of listings. Prices are shown in naira (₦)[1].

AI assistants

What it is: a private place on a plan for chat, writing and image tools[1].

Who it suits: people who want to try a paid tier of an AI tool without carrying the full single-person price.

Check first: the provider’s rules. OpenAI’s consumer terms say you may not share your account credentials or make your account available to anyone else[7]. Subora’s terms say that some AI and software providers do not allow sharing, that each vendor must check those rules, and that a provider can change or end access at any time[4]. If you need the tool for important work, our guide to paying for ChatGPT and other dollar subscriptions explains the alternative of paying for your own account.

Streaming and music

What it is: one screen or one seat on a family or premium plan[1].

Who it suits: someone who wants a single place on a plan built for several people.

Check first: how the plan is shared. Spotify’s Premium for Family terms, for example, require every member to live at the same physical address as the plan owner[8]. Read the vendor’s rules of use on the listing, which you accept before you pay[1], and ask in chat if anything is unclear.

Design and software

What it is: team seats for tools used at work and school[1].

Who it suits: students, freelancers and small teams who need one seat of a tool rather than a whole plan.

Check first: how long the seat is meant to last. If you will rely on it for a project or a semester, ask the vendor what happens if the provider changes the plan part-way through. The refund rules apply when a provider ends access[4], but a refund does not finish your project.

Courses and templates

What it is: recorded lessons, project files, templates and content packs[1].

Who it suits: learners who want a specific skill or a ready-made starting file.

Check first: that the description matches what you need. A course or download is refundable if the files do not open or do not match the description, as long as you report it within 48 hours[3]. That makes the first two days the time to open every file.

Accounts and numbers

What it is: aged social accounts and virtual numbers, with extra checks on every seller[1].

Who it suits: people with a specific need for an account or a number who understand the risk.

Check first: this is the highest-risk group, so the rules are the strictest. Sellers go through identity verification, a video selfie, a proof of address and a ₦100,000 security deposit[2]. An account can be claimed within 24 hours of handover, and an OTP number is refunded automatically if no SMS arrives within 20 minutes[2][3]. The platforms these accounts belong to set their own rules, and Subora’s terms make each vendor responsible for checking them[4].

A note on gift cards and crypto

Subora’s safety and refund pages also set rules for gift cards and crypto, and sellers of these need asset seller status[1][2]. Gift cards must be reported within 30 minutes of revealing the code. Crypto is refunded in full if nothing arrives within 1 hour of payment or the wrong amount is sent[3]. These are not in the five-group list above, so check the marketplace to see whether any are listed today.

What this guide cannot tell you: what is in stock today

A named category does not mean there is a listing in it right now. Subora works with a small number of vendors approved by hand[5], so some categories can be busy and others quiet. We would rather say that plainly than promise something you cannot find.

The reliable way to find out is to look. Open the marketplace, search for the tool or course you want, and compare the listings you find. On each listing you can read the vendor’s terms, choose a plan length and ask the vendor questions before you pay[1]. If the thing you want is not listed, nothing has been lost, and you have not paid anything.

The thing to understand about shared plans

Shared subscriptions are the heart of what Subora sells, and they carry one risk that escrow cannot remove. Providers write their own rules. Some forbid sharing logins, as OpenAI’s terms do[7]. Others allow sharing only in limited ways, as Spotify’s family terms do[8]. Subora’s terms say the same thing from the marketplace side: some providers do not allow sharing, vendors must check before they list, and a provider may end access at any time[4].

What escrow does is protect your money. You pay into escrow, and the vendor is paid only after you confirm your access works, or after 48 hours if you have not reported a problem[2]. If access stops, you report it and the refund rules apply[3]. What escrow does not do is guarantee that a provider will accept the arrangement.

So match the category to the stakes. A seat you use for casual streaming is low risk. An account you depend on for your job or your studies deserves more thought, and sometimes your own subscription is the better choice. Our guide to digital product scams and escrow covers the warning signs to look for in a seller.

Which category fits what you need

  • “I want to try a paid AI tool.” Look at AI assistants. Ask how access is delivered and what happens if the provider ends it.
  • “I want to watch or listen without paying for a full plan.” Look at streaming and music. Check the rules of use on the listing.
  • “I need a tool for school or a project.” Look at design and software. Check the plan length covers your deadline.
  • “I want to learn a skill or save time with a template.” Look at courses and templates. Open every file within 48 hours.
  • “I need an account or a number.” Look at accounts and numbers, and read the claim window before you pay.

How buying works, in five steps

  1. Browse and compare. No account is needed to look[1].
  2. Create a free account. Sign up with your email and phone number and confirm with a one-time code[1].
  3. Fund your wallet. Pay by card, bank transfer or USSD. Everything on Subora is paid from this wallet[1].
  4. Read the vendor’s terms, pick a plan length and pay into escrow. Longer plans cost less per month on most subscriptions[1]. Your money is held, not sent.
  5. Test your access and confirm. The vendor is paid once you confirm, or after 48 hours with no problem reported[1]. If something is wrong, tap Report a problem[2].

Buying is free. You pay only the price shown on the listing[5]. For a deeper check of any single listing, read our guide on how to check a digital product listing before you buy.

Frequently asked questions

What can I buy on Subora?

Subora’s listings fall into five groups: AI assistants, streaming and music, design and software, courses and templates, and accounts and numbers[1]. Gift cards and crypto are also covered by its safety rules[2]. What is listed on any given day depends on which vendors are active, so check the marketplace[6].

Do I need an account to look at listings?

No. Anyone can browse and compare prices. You need a free account when you are ready to buy or chat with a vendor[1].

Are prices in naira?

Yes. Prices are shown in Nigerian naira (₦), and you fund your wallet by card, bank transfer or USSD[1].

Who sells on Subora?

A small number of vendors approved by hand after identity checks, plus Subora’s own official listings[5].

Is it allowed to share a subscription?

It depends on the provider. Some forbid it[7], and some allow it only under conditions[8]. Subora’s terms say vendors must check each provider’s rules before listing[4].

What if my access stops working?

Tap Report a problem on your subscription card. The vendor has 48 hours to respond while your payment stays frozen in escrow. If you disagree, Subora decides from the chat and payment record[2].
