How to pay for ChatGPT, Netflix and other dollar subscriptions in Nigeria (2026 guide)

ChatGPT, Netflix and most AI and design tools bill in dollars, but a Nigerian bank card does not always cooperate. This guide shows what you will really pay, why payments fail, and the safest ways to get access.

By Subora Editorial Team · Published 30 October 2026 · Updated 30 October 2026 · 7 min read

Key takeaways

  • ChatGPT Plus is priced at $20 a month, billed monthly. OpenAI’s Help Center states the price in dollars[1].
  • Nigeria charges 7.5% VAT on digital services from foreign companies. OpenAI began collecting it from 1 November 2025[2], so $20 becomes about $21.50 at checkout.
  • Naira cards were widely blocked for foreign payments in 2022. Some banks switched them back on in 2025, with limits[4][5]. Check your own bank before you assume a card will fail or work.
  • Budget for the checkout total plus a few percent for your bank’s exchange rate and any card fees.
  • Providers such as OpenAI forbid sharing logins[10]. For anything important, pay for your own account.

What you actually pay

OpenAI’s Help Center lists ChatGPT Plus at $20 a month, billed monthly. It also says that Plus has no annual billing and no option to pay several months in advance[1]. That is the price in dollars. The naira amount depends on the exchange rate on the day you pay and on what your checkout page shows.

Tax is the second part. Nigeria applies 7.5% VAT to digital services supplied by foreign companies. OpenAI started collecting it from Nigerian customers on 1 November 2025, joining platforms such as Netflix, Google, Amazon and Meta that already charged it[2]. Under the Nigeria Tax Act 2025, the rules for non-resident digital suppliers apply from 1 January 2026 and the VAT rate stays at 7.5%[3]. The amount you see on the provider’s checkout page is the one that counts.

Here is a worked example for ChatGPT Plus. It uses ₦1,333.65 per dollar, the official-market rate reported for 20 October 2026[7]. It is an illustration, not a quote.

Your bank or card provider will not charge exactly that rate. They add their own spread and sometimes a fee. One virtual card provider publishes a $0.35 transaction fee plus a 2% exchange-rate spread[9]. On ₦28,673, a 2% spread is about ₦573. Check the live official rate on the Central Bank of Nigeria’s site[8], then add a few percent for the real cost.

Why Nigerian cards get declined

The old advice says naira cards never work abroad. That was close to true for a while. In March 2022, some of the biggest banks cut the monthly international limit on naira debit cards to as low as $20[6]. GTBank told customers that its naira Mastercard would stop working for international online and point-of-sale payments from 31 December 2022[4].

Then the position changed. In July 2025, GTBank, UBA and Wema Bank began switching international use back on for naira cards, with limits[5]. So the honest answer today is “it depends on your bank, your card type and your limit”.

If your payment still fails, these are the usual causes. They come from how card payments work in general, so check each one:

  • International or online payments are switched off. Look for a card controls or limits screen in your banking app.
  • You have reached the monthly limit. Ask your bank what the current limit is, in dollars.
  • The balance is too low after conversion. The bank needs the naira equivalent of the dollar price plus VAT, plus its own charges.
  • The verification step timed out. Many banks send an OTP or an in-app approval. Keep your phone ready and your network stable.
  • Your bank’s fraud rules flagged a first payment to a foreign merchant. A call to your bank often clears it.

A six-step checklist before you pay

  1. Read the price on the provider’s own checkout page. It shows the tax you will really pay.
  2. Switch on international payments in your banking app and ask for your current limit.
  3. Fund the card with a cushion. I would keep about 5% more than the naira total you expect, to cover the rate and fees.
  4. Pay through a licensed bank or card provider. Never send money to someone who offers to “pay for you”. See the red flags in our guide to digital product scams in Nigeria.
  5. Keep the receipt and set a renewal reminder. With OpenAI you manage or cancel the plan from Settings, then Account[1].
  6. If you were charged but still see the free plan, use OpenAI’s troubleshooting steps. Approved refunds are typically processed within 5 to 10 business days[1].

Shared subscriptions: what providers say

Many Nigerians split the cost of a plan, and shared listings are common online. Before you join one, know the rules. OpenAI’s Terms of Use say you may not share your account credentials or make your account available to anyone else[10]. Other providers write their own rules. Some allow household or extra-member features, and many do not.

Subora’s own safety page says the same: some streaming, AI and software providers do not allow account sharing or reselling, and each vendor must check those rules before listing[11]. The practical risks are a suspended account with no refund from the provider, lost history, and access that stops mid-month.

To lower your risk:

  • Pay for your own account when the work matters, such as client files, private data or business use.
  • Never put sensitive information into an account that someone else also controls.
  • Prefer arrangements that fit the provider’s rules, such as an invitation to a plan that allows extra members, over a shared password.
  • Pay only inside an escrow flow, test your access straight away, and report problems inside the claim window[11].

Where escrow fits in

On Subora, you fund a wallet by card, bank transfer or USSD, pay for a listing in naira, and your money is held in escrow. The vendor is paid after you confirm that your access works, or after 48 hours if you have not reported a problem[12]. If access stops working, you can report it on your subscription card. The vendor then has 48 hours to respond, and your payment stays frozen while they do[11].

Escrow protects your money if a seller does not deliver. It does not change a provider’s terms, and it does not help if you pay a seller directly outside the platform. That is why the 48-hour window matters: test your access as soon as you get it.

Mistakes that cost Nigerians money

  • Paying a stranger’s bank account for “cheap ChatGPT”. There is no escrow, no refund path, and no way to check who you paid.
  • Buying “lifetime” access to a monthly service. A monthly product cannot be sold for life without a catch.
  • Sharing an OTP or password. Subora staff never ask for your password or codes[11], and neither should anyone selling you a subscription.
  • Budgeting only the dollar price. VAT, the exchange rate and card fees all add to the naira total.
  • Leaving a virtual card funded. Top up only what the next payment needs, and cancel plans you no longer use.

Frequently asked questions

Can I pay for ChatGPT Plus with a naira card?

Sometimes. Several Nigerian banks switched international use back on for naira cards in 2025, with limits[5], but others did not. Check your bank’s current policy and your monthly dollar limit. If the payment is declined, use a dollar card or a virtual dollar card.

How much is ChatGPT Plus in naira?

OpenAI lists the price as $20 a month[1], plus 7.5% VAT in Nigeria[2]. The naira total depends on the exchange rate your bank applies. At the official-market rate of ₦1,333.65 per dollar on 20 October 2026[7], $21.50 comes to about ₦28,673 before bank charges.

Does ChatGPT Plus have an annual plan?

OpenAI’s Help Center says Plus does not currently support annual billing or paying several months in advance[1]. Check OpenAI’s pricing page for any change.

Is it safe to use a virtual dollar card?

It can be, if the provider is licensed and transparent about its fees. Compare the funding spread, card creation fee and monthly charges[9]. Fund the card only for the payment you are about to make.

Can I share a ChatGPT account with a friend?

OpenAI’s Terms of Use say you may not share your credentials or make your account available to anyone else[10]. If a friend needs ChatGPT, they should sign up for their own account.

What if I was charged but did not get Plus?

Sign in with the same method you used to subscribe, then follow OpenAI’s troubleshooting steps. If you still need help, you can request a refund[1].
