Escrow vs paying a seller directly on WhatsApp: what you lose without protection

A seller on WhatsApp says, “Just send it to my account and I will send your login.” It feels quick and friendly. It also removes every protection you would have inside a marketplace. This guide compares the two ways of paying side by side, explains why some sellers push you outside, and says what to do if you have already paid.

By Subora Editorial Team · Published 5 November 2026 · Updated 5 November 2026 · 7 min read

Key takeaways

  • Paying a seller directly means your money leaves your account before you know the product works. Escrow reverses the order: the money is held until you confirm.
  • On Subora, payments made outside the platform have no escrow, no refunds and no dispute help[1][3].
  • With escrow, the vendor is paid when you confirm that your access works, or after 48 hours with no problem reported[2].
  • Social engineering, which means tricking people, was the most common technique in Nigeria’s bank and payment fraud data for 2025. That data is not a count of WhatsApp sales scams[4].
  • If you have already paid a seller outside a platform, call your bank first, keep every screenshot, then report to the EFCC[5] and the FCCPC[6].

The short answer

Paying a seller directly on WhatsApp is a trust decision with nothing behind it. You send the money first and hope the seller then sends what you paid for. Escrow is the opposite arrangement. A third party holds your payment while you test the product, and the seller is paid only once you confirm that it works, or once a set time passes with no complaint[2].

Both can end well when the seller is honest. The difference is what happens when the seller is not, or when the product simply fails. This guide is editorial advice from our team. The rules it describes for Subora are Subora’s published rules.

What paying a seller directly looks like

The pattern is usually simple. You see an offer, perhaps a cheap streaming plan, a course or an AI subscription. You message the seller. They give you an account number and ask you to pay, then promise to send a login or a code. The chat is private, the terms are whatever was said in messages, and the only record is whatever you saved yourself.

Nothing in this arrangement is illegal or even unusual. It is how many honest small sellers work. The problem is structural. Once you send the money, you have no way to test the product first, no neutral person to ask for help, and no process for a refund. Your only tools are the seller’s goodwill and your bank. If the seller goes quiet, you are left asking for a favour, not claiming a right.

What escrow does instead

On Subora, you pay from a wallet you fund yourself, by card, bank transfer or USSD. When you pay for a listing, Subora holds the money instead of sending it to the vendor[2]. Your access appears on your subscription card. You test it. The vendor is paid when you confirm, or after 48 hours if you have not reported a problem[1].

If something is wrong, you tap Report a problem. The vendor has 48 hours to respond, and your payment stays frozen in escrow meanwhile. A refund can be full, for the days you did not use, or a custom amount, and it returns to your Subora wallet straight away. If you and the vendor disagree, Subora decides using the full chat and payment record and writes down the reason[1]. For the full process, read how escrow works, step by step.

Why some sellers want you to pay outside

A seller who steers you away from the platform gains three things. They get paid immediately, with no wait for your confirmation. They avoid a dispute record that could be used against them. And they avoid the review that listings go through before they appear. None of this proves bad intent, but all of it works in the seller’s favour and not yours.

Some will say the platform costs you extra, so paying direct is cheaper. On Subora, buying is free and you pay the price shown on the listing[7]. Vendors pay the fee, not buyers. That argument therefore does not hold.

On Subora, any chat message that asks you to pay or talk outside the platform can be reported. Those messages are hidden and reviewed[1]. Subora staff never ask for your password, your codes or payment outside the app[1]. Treat the same behaviour from a seller as a red flag. Our guide to digital product scams and escrow lists nine red flags.

It helps to know the wider picture. Nigeria’s inter-bank payments body reported that social engineering was the most common fraud technique in 2025[4]. That figure covers bank and payment fraud across the industry. It does not count WhatsApp sales scams, so treat it as the backdrop, not the size of this particular problem.

What escrow does not do

Escrow protects your payment for what the listing promised. It is not a promise about everything else. Three limits are worth knowing.

  • It does not change the provider’s rules. Some services, such as streaming, AI and software companies, have their own rules about sharing accounts, and a provider can change or end access at any time. If that happens, the refund rules apply[1].
  • It depends on you testing quickly. The vendor is paid after 48 hours with no problem reported, so test your access as soon as it arrives and report at once if it fails[2].
  • It only covers payments made through the platform. If you pay a vendor outside Subora, there is no escrow, no refund and no dispute help[3].

If you have already paid a seller directly

Act in this order, and act quickly.

  1. Call your bank first. Tell them you sent money to a seller who has not delivered. Banks can only help in some cases, but the sooner you call, the better.
  2. Keep every record. Save screenshots of the offer, the chat, the account details you were given and your payment receipt before anything is deleted.
  3. Report it. The Economic and Financial Crimes Commission takes complaints through several official channels[5]. For a dispute with a business that is not resolved, the Federal Competition and Consumer Protection Commission takes complaints through its website[6].
  4. Do not pay more to “release” the order. A second payment demanded to unlock the first is a common pressure tactic.

A simple rule to remember

If the money leaves your hands before you can test the product, you are relying on trust alone. For a first purchase, from a seller you do not know, that is a poor trade for a small saving. Pay inside a platform that holds the money, test straight away, and confirm only when it works. To see how the full buying flow fits together, read how Subora works and buying safely on Subora.

Frequently asked questions

Is it safe to pay a seller directly on WhatsApp?

It can work with an honest seller, but you have no protection if it does not. Your money leaves your account before you can test the product, and there is no neutral process for a refund or a dispute. Paying through a platform that holds the money until you confirm is safer.

What is escrow in simple terms?

Escrow means a third party holds your payment until you confirm that you received what you paid for. On Subora, the vendor is paid when you confirm that your access works, or after 48 hours with no problem reported[2].

Why can I not pay a vendor directly on Subora?

You pay for everything from your Subora wallet. Payments made outside Subora have no escrow, no refunds and no dispute help, because they do not pass through Subora’s escrow[3].

Can I get my money back if I paid a seller outside Subora?

Not through Subora. Call your bank straight away, keep all screenshots and receipts, and report to the EFCC[5] and the FCCPC[6].

Does escrow protect me if the provider ends access?

A provider can change or end access at any time. If that happens, Subora’s refund rules apply[1]. Escrow protects your payment, but it cannot make a provider allow sharing.

What should I do if a seller asks me to move to WhatsApp?

Stay in the platform chat and keep payment inside the platform. On Subora you can report a message that asks you to pay or talk outside, and it will be hidden and reviewed[1].
